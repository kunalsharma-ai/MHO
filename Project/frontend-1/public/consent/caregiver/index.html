Welcome & membership

1. Welcome to Our Caregivers Panel

Welcome to the Frequent Research Caregivers Panel, a research community for individuals who provide care, assistance, supervision or support to other people.

Our panel welcomes eligible caregivers from different backgrounds, including:

Professional caregivers and paid care workers.

Home-care assistants and personal care workers.

Nursing aides and healthcare support workers.

Elderly care and senior support providers.

Disability support workers and personal support assistants.

Childcare providers and eligible childcare professionals.

Family caregivers and informal caregivers.

Parents and guardians who provide care for dependants.

Respite caregivers and community support workers.

Residential care and assisted-living support staff.

Other individuals who provide eligible caregiving services.

Our research may explore caregiving responsibilities, care routines, support needs, care products, healthcare services, assistive technology, working conditions, caregiving challenges and the experiences of people who provide or receive care.

Participation may involve online surveys, interviews, opinion polls, product evaluations, focus groups or other research activities.

Participation is voluntary. Joining the panel does not establish a healthcare, employment, legal, financial or advisory relationship with Frequent Research or its clients.

2. Eligibility and Membership

To join the panel, you must meet the applicable minimum age requirement and provide accurate registration information.

Depending on the study, eligibility may require:

Experience providing care to a child, older adult, person with a disability or another individual who needs assistance.

Employment or professional experience in a caregiving role.

Responsibility for regular or occasional caregiving activities.

Experience with specific care settings, products, services or support needs.

Compliance with any additional eligibility requirements disclosed for a particular study.

Some studies may be intended for professional caregivers, while others may be designed for family members or informal caregivers. You must meet the criteria for the relevant study.

Professional qualifications, licences or registration details will only be required where relevant to the study.

You must not falsely claim caregiving experience, professional credentials or responsibilities that you do not have.

We may verify relevant information using proportionate and lawful methods. Membership does not guarantee eligibility for every study or a minimum number of survey invitations.

3. Information We May Collect

We may collect information relevant to panel membership, research eligibility and the purposes explained in our Privacy Notice.

A. Registration and contact information

Full name or preferred display name.

Email address and telephone number.

Country, state, city or general location.

Preferred language and communication preferences.

Age or date of birth where necessary to establish eligibility.

Account credentials and security information.

B. Caregiving profile information

Depending on your role and the study, we may collect:

Type of caregiving role: professional, paid, family or informal.

General caregiving experience and duration.

Care setting, such as home, residential facility, community setting or workplace.

General caregiving responsibilities and frequency of care.

Broad age category of the person receiving care.

General categories of support provided, such as mobility assistance, daily living activities, supervision or companionship.

Employment status, professional role and relevant qualifications.

General information about care-related products, services and support resources.

Household or family caregiving arrangements where relevant.

We will seek only information reasonably necessary for the stated research purpose.

C. Survey and research information

We may collect your responses concerning:

Caregiving routines and experiences.

Challenges, time commitments and support needs.

Use and evaluation of care products or assistive technology.

Experiences with healthcare, community or support services.

Care-related purchasing decisions and preferences.

Training, working conditions and professional development.

Opinions about care policies, services and improvements.

General experiences of people receiving care, where appropriately anonymised.

You should answer based on your genuine experience and avoid including information that could identify a person receiving care.

D. Technical and security information

We may collect IP address, browser and device details, login timestamps, session information, essential cookies, survey progress and response-quality indicators.

We may use proportionate security measures to protect accounts, detect duplicate participation and prevent fraudulent submissions.

Optional analytics and tracking technologies will be handled according to applicable law and your cookie preferences.

E. Incentive and payment information

Where a study offers points, gift cards, vouchers, cash incentives or other rewards, we may collect the information needed to administer and verify the reward.

Tax or identity verification information will be requested only where appropriate and legally required.

F. Sensitive personal information

Caregiving studies may involve sensitive information relating to health, disability, family circumstances or other specially protected matters.

We will not collect such information routinely unless it is relevant to a legitimate research purpose. Where required, we will provide additional information and obtain explicit consent or another appropriate lawful authorisation.

Joining the panel does not automatically authorise the collection of detailed medical records, diagnoses, disability records or other sensitive information.

4. How We Use Your Information

We may use your information to:

Register and administer your panel account.

Understand your caregiving background and determine study eligibility.

Match you with relevant research opportunities.

Conduct research into caregiving experiences, products, services and support needs.

Analyse trends and identify opportunities to improve care-related products and services.

Validate responses and protect research quality.

Administer eligible incentives and payments.

Communicate research invitations, account updates and support information.

Improve our panel services and research processes.

Prevent fraud, unauthorised access and misuse of the panel.

Meet applicable legal, regulatory and contractual obligations.

We will not use your information for a materially different purpose without an appropriate lawful basis and, where required, providing notice or obtaining additional consent.

5. Voluntary Participation

Your participation in the panel and in individual studies is voluntary.

You may decline invitations and, where the study permits, skip questions or stop participating.

You agree to:

Provide truthful responses based on your own experience.

Participate only in studies for which you are eligible.

Follow study instructions and applicable confidentiality requirements.

Avoid misrepresenting your caregiving responsibilities or qualifications.

Avoid submitting fabricated experiences or automated responses.

Respect the privacy and dignity of people receiving care.

You do not have to participate in every survey or accept every research invitation.

Participation does not guarantee that your views will influence a product, service, policy or business decision.

6. Privacy of People Receiving Care

Protecting the privacy, dignity and confidentiality of care recipients is a fundamental condition of panel participation.

When describing your caregiving experiences, you must not submit identifying information about another person unless the specific study is authorised to collect it and all applicable legal and consent requirements have been met.

Unless expressly authorised through an appropriate study process, do not include:

Full names, personal addresses or telephone numbers.

Medical record numbers or other identification numbers.

Photographs, videos or audio recordings that identify a care recipient.

Medical reports, prescriptions or clinical records.

Detailed descriptions that could reasonably identify an individual.

Confidential information about a child, older adult, patient or person with a disability.

Private information about family members, clients or people in your care.

Use general descriptions, age ranges and anonymised examples wherever possible.

Removing a person’s name alone may not be sufficient to prevent identification. Consider whether other details could identify the individual when combined.

If you accidentally submit identifiable information, notify the research team promptly. We may restrict access, remove or securely handle the information as appropriate.

7. Special Protection for Children and Vulnerable Individuals

Some caregiving studies may concern children, older adults, people with disabilities or individuals who require assistance with decision-making.

You must respect their privacy, dignity, autonomy and applicable legal rights.

Unless a study has an appropriate, lawful process for collecting such information, you must not submit identifiable details about a child or vulnerable person.

Where a study involves information about a child or another person who may lack the legal capacity to consent, the required parental, guardian or legally authorised representative permission and any additional safeguards must be established before that information is collected.

You must not assume that being a family member, caregiver or guardian automatically authorises every type of research disclosure.

Participation in our general panel does not replace any consent, safeguarding or ethics approval required for a particular study.

8. Professional Caregiver Responsibilities

If you provide paid or professional care, you must comply with the legal, professional, contractual and ethical obligations applicable to your role.

You must not disclose confidential client information, workplace records or restricted care information without appropriate authority.

You are responsible for ensuring that your participation does not interfere with your professional duties or compromise the safety, dignity or privacy of people receiving care.

Participation does not authorise you to use confidential workplace information, provide unauthorised medical advice or represent the views of your employer.

If a study conflicts with your professional obligations, you should decline the relevant question or contact the research team.

9. Family and Informal Caregivers

Family and informal caregivers may participate in eligible studies based on their own caregiving experiences.

You should describe your personal experiences without disclosing unnecessary private information about the person receiving care.

Where a question concerns another person’s medical condition, financial circumstances or other sensitive information, provide only the level of detail necessary and permitted by the study.

You may decline to answer questions that make you uncomfortable, subject to the relevant study’s eligibility and completion rules.

Participation does not make you responsible for providing medical advice or professional care beyond your existing role.

10. Research Quality and Verification

We may use proportionate quality checks to identify duplicate accounts, inconsistent responses, false eligibility claims, automated submissions or suspected fraud.

We may request clarification or verification where reasonably necessary.

Where a response is invalid or an account appears to violate the panel rules, we may investigate and take proportionate action, including excluding a submission or suspending an account, where justified and permitted by law.

A screening-out decision does not necessarily mean that you have acted improperly. Studies may have limited quotas or specific eligibility requirements.

Where practical, we will provide a way to raise questions about disputed decisions.

11. Incentives, Rewards and Payments

Eligible studies may offer points, cash incentives, gift cards, vouchers or other rewards.

The study invitation will explain the incentive and completion requirements.

Rewards are subject to the stated eligibility and validation conditions.

Payment methods and processing times may vary by country and study.

You must provide accurate redemption information.

We may investigate suspected fraudulent or duplicate reward claims.

Any withholding or adjustment of an incentive must be justified and handled according to the published rules and applicable law.

Tax obligations will be handled according to applicable requirements.

Participation does not guarantee a minimum number of surveys or a particular income.

Reward thresholds, expiry periods, redemption methods and processing timelines will be described in the applicable Reward Policy or study invitation.

12. Sharing Information with Research Clients and Service Providers

We may conduct research for organisations involved in caregiving, healthcare, disability support, elderly care, childcare, assistive technology, care products, community services and related industries.

Where necessary and lawful, information may be shared with:

Research clients and authorised research partners.

Survey platforms and panel management providers.

Cloud hosting and technical support providers.

Security and fraud-prevention providers.

Incentive and payment processing providers.

Professional advisers, auditors or public authorities where legally required.

We apply appropriate safeguards to information handled by service providers on our behalf.

Where possible and appropriate, research findings will be reported in aggregated, statistical or appropriately anonymised form.

If a study involves sharing your identity, identifiable quotations or information about your professional role with a client, we will explain the purpose and obtain any additional consent required.

Joining the panel does not automatically authorise disclosure of your identifiable information to unrelated businesses for their own marketing.

13. Audio, Video, Interviews and Photographs

Some studies may involve recorded interviews, online focus groups, video calls, photographs, product demonstrations or other recorded activities.

Before recording begins, we will explain the purpose, the type of recording, the intended recipients, the proposed use and the applicable retention arrangements.

We will obtain separate consent where required.

You must not record or share images, audio, video or other identifiable material involving a care recipient without the required authority and consent.

Declining an optional recorded activity will not automatically affect unrelated panel participation. An unrecorded alternative may not be available for every study.

14. Privacy, Cookies and Technical Information

We will handle your personal information in accordance with our Privacy Notice and applicable data protection laws.

Essential cookies and similar technologies may be used for login sessions, security and website functionality.

Where required, optional analytics or tracking technologies will be activated only after obtaining appropriate consent. You may manage available preferences through our cookie settings.

We may use proportionate security measures to protect accounts, detect duplicate participation and prevent fraudulent activity.

Additional information and permissions will be provided where required for sensitive personal data, optional tracking or other specially protected information.

15. Your Privacy Rights

Depending on your country and applicable law, you may have the right to:

Access your personal information.

Correct inaccurate or incomplete information.

Request deletion of eligible personal information.

Withdraw consent where processing is based on consent.

Object to or restrict certain processing.

Request a portable copy of certain information.

Opt out of optional marketing communications.

Manage optional cookie preferences.

Complain to the relevant data protection authority.

To exercise your rights, contact support@myhealthopinions.com.

We may need to verify your identity before responding. Some information may be retained where legally required or where another lawful basis applies, such as accounting, security, legal claims or dispute resolution.

16. Communications and Research Invitations

We may send essential communications relating to account security, membership, password changes, reward processing, privacy notices and important service updates.

We may also send research invitations according to your preferences and applicable law.

Optional promotional communications will be handled separately where required. You may opt out of optional marketing without necessarily losing panel membership.

17. Account Security and Acceptable Use

You must keep your login credentials secure, provide accurate information and notify us if you suspect unauthorised account access.

You must not:

Create unauthorised duplicate accounts.

Misrepresent your caregiving experience or professional qualifications.

Use bots or automated tools to submit survey responses.

Manipulate screening, eligibility or reward systems.

Submit unlawful, infringing or malicious material.

Disclose confidential research materials without permission.

Upload identifiable information about care recipients without appropriate authority.

Harass participants, researchers, clients or support staff.

Use the panel to obtain private information about people receiving care.

We may investigate suspected violations and take proportionate action, including rejecting affected submissions, restricting rewards or suspending accounts where justified and permitted by law.

18. Intellectual Property and Research Materials

Survey questions, research designs, client materials, software and other supplied research content may be protected by intellectual property rights or confidentiality obligations.

You may use such materials only for the authorised purpose of participating in the relevant study.

Unless authorised, you must not reproduce, publish, distribute or disclose confidential study materials.

You retain rights in your personal information as provided by applicable law. Any permission relating to comments or other materials you submit will be limited to the purposes described in the relevant study notice and applicable agreement.

19. Withdrawal and Account Closure

You may stop participating in research and request account closure at any time, subject to applicable law and the practical requirements of a study already completed.

We will delete or anonymise eligible personal information according to our retention policy and applicable law.

Certain records may need to be retained for legal, accounting, security or dispute-resolution purposes.

Pending rewards and redemptions will be handled according to the published reward rules and applicable law.

You may withdraw a particular consent separately where processing is based on consent. Withdrawal does not invalidate processing that was lawful before withdrawal.

20. Data Retention and International Transfers

We retain personal information only for as long as reasonably necessary for the stated purposes, subject to applicable legal, contractual and operational requirements.

Retention periods may differ for account information, survey responses, reward transactions, consent records and security logs.

Our research may involve clients and service providers in different countries. Where information is transferred internationally, we will apply appropriate safeguards where required and provide relevant information about those arrangements.

We maintain reasonable technical and organisational safeguards designed to protect personal information. No online system can be guaranteed to be completely secure.

21. Eligibility, Minors and Safeguarding

The general Caregivers Panel is intended for participants who meet the applicable minimum age requirement.

Where a study permits participation by minors or involves research concerning children, appropriate parental or guardian authorisation, age-appropriate information and any additional safeguards required by law must be implemented.

Caregiving responsibilities do not automatically establish legal authority to consent to research on behalf of another individual.

Any study involving vulnerable participants must follow the applicable legal, safeguarding and ethical requirements.

22. Changes to These Terms

We may update these terms to reflect changes in our services, research practices, security arrangements or legal requirements.

Where a change materially affects your rights or how your personal information is used, we will provide appropriate notice and obtain renewed consent where required.

Continued use of the panel will not replace a separate consent where the law requires affirmative agreement.

23. Service Availability, Liability and Complaints

We will take reasonable steps to maintain our panel services, but temporary interruptions may occur because of maintenance, technical issues, security incidents or circumstances outside our reasonable control.

Nothing in these terms excludes or limits any right, remedy or liability that cannot lawfully be excluded or limited. Any permitted limitations will be interpreted according to applicable law.

For questions, complaints or requests, contact:

We will review complaints and respond in accordance with applicable requirements.

Your permissions apply only to the purposes described in the relevant notice and the options you select. Optional permissions may be declined or withdrawn where applicable without automatically cancelling your general panel membership.
